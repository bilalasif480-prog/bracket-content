## Hook formulas that work

Distilled from the reels above (visual hooks) and turned into Bracket formulas. Each one keeps the *mechanism* and uses Bracket's own screen recordings, mock UIs, Bilal's face or his own b-roll.

### 1. The "about to break" frame (stress hook)
- **Why it works:** frame 0 shows something one second away from going wrong; viewers stay to see if it does. This was the top mechanism on the account (6M, 1.1M, 302K, 133K).
- **Frame-0 design:** the problem is already happening in motion, with no title. At most one 3–6-word line in the top third.
- **Bracket versions:**
  - A phone screen of a slow café website, with the loading spinner turning while a thumb hovers over "back". Line: "Your customer is about to leave."
  - A website contact form where the "Send" button is pressed and nothing happens, and the cursor shakes. Line: "This form just lost you a booking."

### 2. Countdown → payoff (anticipation hook)
- **Why it works:** a visible countdown or "press the button" moment promises a payoff on a timer (285K, 84K, 847K).
- **Frame-0 design:** a finger over a button, or a timer already running at "0:10". The payoff lands by 6–8 s.
- **Bracket versions:**
  - A finger over "Publish" on a new site, with the timer "3…2…1" and then the live site on a phone. Line: "Launching a Manchester café's new site." Use this only with a real project and the client's OK; otherwise label it "demo site".
  - A stopwatch next to a WhatsApp chat: a customer message arrives, and the AI agent's draft reply appears. Line: "How fast can an AI agent reply? (demo)". Show the real time from the recording; never invent a number.

### 3. Challenge question
- **Why it works:** one plain question over an obvious test makes the viewer play along (847K: "Who can escape…?").
- **Frame-0 design:** a single question in small white sentence-case text, top third, over a moving test.
- **Bracket versions:**
  - "Can you book a table on this website in under 10 seconds?" over a screen recording of a messy demo site.
  - "Which of these two shop pages would you buy from?" in a split screen: a cluttered page vs a clean Bracket demo.

### 4. Expectation vs reality (bait-and-switch)
- **Why it works:** frame 0 sets up one story and the payoff flips it (725K: a fake crash turns into a pancake delivery).
- **Frame-0 design:** start on the "bad" scene in full motion; the switch comes by 3–5 s.
- **Bracket versions:**
  - It opens on a scary red "Not secure" browser warning on a business site, then flips to the same page fixed with a padlock. Line: "Expectation: expensive fix. Reality:" Then show the real care-plan price (£43/month) only as listed in business/bracket.md.
  - It opens on an owner drowning in DMs (a pile-up of phone notifications), then flips to the same DMs answered by an AI agent (demo). Line: "Expectation: hire someone. Reality:"

### 5. Split-screen reaction
- **Why it works:** action on top and a human face reacting below; the face tells you to keep watching (179K).
- **Frame-0 design:** a 50/50 split. Top: a screen recording already moving. Bottom: Bilal's face mid-reaction. No text.
- **Bracket versions:**
  - Top: Bilal types "best café in Ancoats" into ChatGPT and the answer loads. Bottom: Bilal watches to see if the café appears. Line after 2 s: "Is your business in the AI answer?" (GEO)
  - Top: Google search for a trade in Manchester, scrolling past the map pack. Bottom: Bilal winces. "This is why your Google Business Profile matters." (SEO)

### 6. Pattern interrupt
- **Why it works:** something odd that doesn't belong breaks autoplay scrolling (847K, someone biting cardboard).
- **Frame-0 design:** an unexpected action or object in the first 0.5 s; the explanation comes after.
- **Bracket versions:**
  - Bilal tears a printed paper menu in half on camera. Line: "Your menu shouldn't live on paper (or a PDF)."
  - Bilal drops an old flip phone into a bin. Line: "If your website looks like this phone…"

### 7. Satisfying build / reveal
- **Why it works:** a smooth, oddly satisfying motion holds viewers to the finish (302K, pizza pull).
- **Frame-0 design:** the motion has already started (blocks snapping in, a page assembling). No title for the first 1 s.
- **Bracket versions:**
  - A website assembling block by block in 7 s (use the build-reel template `single-reels/2026-11-02-build-block-by-block/reel10.js`). Line at 1 s: "Building a website for a Manchester barber (demo)."
  - App screens sliding into a phone frame one by one. Line: "Turning a booking form into an app, in 8 seconds."

### 8. Relatable meme caption
- **Why it works:** an unrelated clip plus a caption about the viewer's own life makes it shareable (302K: "How my trading entries be #Precise").
- **Frame-0 design:** a white caption bar (black text) under or over a funny or precise clip, readable in 1 s.
- **Bracket versions:**
  - Over Bilal's own b-roll of a juggling act: "Me answering customer DMs, emails and WhatsApp at 11 pm."
  - Over a clip of someone carefully balancing a stack of cups: "Small business owners updating their own website."

### 9. Danger POV
- **Why it works:** a first-person view of a risky moment (6M bike in rain, 117K train door) makes you feel it.
- **Frame-0 design:** POV phone footage, shaky and real, with the risk visible in the first frame.
- **Bracket versions:**
  - A POV of a customer's thumb on a phone: a site loads with a broken layout and the thumb hovers over a competitor's ad. "POV: your customer, 2 seconds before leaving."
  - A POV of the owner's phone buzzing with a 1-star "couldn't book online" review. Use a mock-up, clearly not a real review. "POV: the booking button doesn't work on mobile."

### 10. One-word label lesson (the account's own payoff)
- **Why it works:** after the clip, a huge one-word label ("STRESS", "ANTICIPATION") names what you just felt, which makes the reel feel like a lesson and gets it saved.
- **Frame-0 design:** this is a mid-reel device, not frame 0: white "THIS is a" + one big yellow word, centred, for about 1 s.
- **Bracket versions:**
  - After the slow-site clip: "THIS is a LOST CUSTOMER."
  - After the AI-agent reply demo: "THIS is an AI AGENT."

## Frame-0 rules (from what we saw)
1. **Motion from the first frame.** The best reels never start on a still, a logo or a title card. Something is already moving.
2. **Tension is visible without sound.** It is all music-only; the hook works on mute. Never rely on the voice-over for the first second.
3. **No headline at frame 0.** The text-list reel ("15 Video formats…") was the lowest at 12.6K. At most: one emoji, OR one short question/caption line (top or bottom third, small, white or a white bar), readable in under 1 s.
4. **Keep the subject in the centre and large:** a phone screen, a face or the "problem" fills the middle of the frame.
5. **Payoff by 5–8 s,** then label it (big centred word: white small lead-in + large yellow/brand-colour word). This fits PM's 26 Nov rule of 7–9 s reels with the hook at 0 s.
6. **Split screen is allowed** when one half is a face reacting.
7. **Never use** other people's viral clips, music or words (this account does; we don't). Use Bracket screen recordings, mock UIs and Bilal-filmed footage.
