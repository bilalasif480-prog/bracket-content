# Board verdict: Bracket (website review, 23 Nov 2026)

**Vote: 3 × FUND IF. Average score 3.7 / 10** (Offers 4, Monopoly 3, Product 4).
Memos: `board/offers.md`, `board/monopoly.md`, `board/product.md`. Brief: `board/brief.md`.

## Risks all three raised
1. **Two markets at once.** The website sells to UK small businesses ("Manchester first"); the profile, Instagram and outreach sell to Kuwait/Gulf, starting with perfume brands. A Kuwait perfume brand lands on an English-only site about Manchester; a Manchester buyer lands on a +965 WhatsApp, Gmail and Gulf-Arabic Instagram. The story breaks at every hand-off.
2. **Five equal services = a menu, not an offer.** Websites, apps and SEO are commodities; with no named result, no starting price and "tell us the job, we quote", buyers can only compare on price.
3. **No proof.** No case studies, testimonials or numbers; one client (payment status unknown); two free demos not converted. Buyers can't believe the result will happen.

Raised by two: capacity (one person with a full-time job) and no repeatable way to win customers yet.

## Conditions (checklist)
- [ ] Pick ONE market. The board leans to Kuwait Instagram/WhatsApp sellers, perfume first; or state clearly why the UK comes first.
- [ ] Make the website, language (English + Gulf Arabic), contact details, payment and content all tell that one story.
- [ ] Lead with ONE product: online store + WhatsApp ordering agent, as a fixed starter package with a delivery time and a starting price (Bilal sets it; `/founder-pricing` can test it).
- [ ] Add a guarantee Bracket can afford (`/founder-cfo` to check it).
- [ ] Move SEO, GEO and apps to add-ons until the lead product has a result.
- [ ] Get proof: written-up results (with permission) from the AC client and one perfume brand.
- [ ] Show the product before the quote: put the demo or the starter package on the site.
- [ ] Write down and test the WhatsApp agent's hand-off to a person (who answers at 2 a.m. or during the day job).
- [ ] Track what each client costs to win and how long it takes.
- [ ] Only real scarcity, based on real weekly capacity.
- [ ] Name what gets harder to copy over time: perfume-store templates, Arabic agent quality, reputation in that niche.

## Where they disagree
- Offers sees the free demo and "you approve" as a low-effort, low-risk start to build on; Product says the demo is hidden in outbound sales and the site itself shows nothing before a quote.
- Product credits the craft (schema, llms.txt, security headers, QA) as a real strength; Monopoly says none of it is defensible, since any AI-assisted competitor can match it.
- Monopoly is the most doubtful (3): even with focus, it wants a reason this can't be copied by month six.

## The strongest version the board can see
Not the business on the website. A Kuwait software house that does one thing for one buyer: an online store plus an Arabic/English WhatsApp ordering agent for Instagram perfume brands that take every order by hand. One starter package with a set price, delivery time and affordable guarantee, sold with a free homepage demo, shown on a bilingual site built for that buyer, and proven with the first two or three documented results before adding other niches or services. The UK site and the five-equal-services menu work against it.
