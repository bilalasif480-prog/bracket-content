# Offers lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A market with real pain and reachable buyers.** Gulf SMBs that run on Instagram and WhatsApp, and perfume brands that take every order by hand in DMs, are easy to find and to message. The pain (lost orders, slow replies) is easy to see from the outside.
- **One part of the stack does more than the others.** "Online store + a WhatsApp agent that answers and books" sells a result (orders taken while you sleep) instead of a deliverable. That is the start of a real offer.
- **Effort is low for the buyer.** A free homepage demo before any commitment, written scope, and "launch after your yes" ask very little of them.
- **Honest trust lines.** "We don't promise rankings" and "you approve" cut perceived risk without fake claims.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No chosen market.** The website sells to Manchester businesses; the sales work and content target Kuwait perfume brands. Two countries, two languages, two price levels, and five services held equal means there is no offer yet, only a menu. A menu competes on price.
2. **Likelihood is close to zero.** No case studies, no numbers, no testimonials, and one client whose payment status is unknown. Buyers can't believe the result will happen, and nothing on offer (no guarantee, no risk reversal) makes up for it.
3. **"Tell us the job, we quote" is a commodity frame.** No named result, no time frame, no stack, no guarantee. Next to freelancers and agencies, the only difference a buyer can see is being cheaper, and one person with a full-time job can't win that race.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one market: Kuwait/Gulf Instagram sellers or UK small businesses? Why that one?
- What result do you sell, in numbers the buyer cares about (orders taken, replies answered, bookings), and how fast does it arrive?
- What does the perfume brand pay today in lost orders or staff time on WhatsApp? Nobody has measured this yet.
- What guarantee could you afford, e.g. "live in X days or you don't pay"?
- How many hours a week can you deliver, and how many clients does that allow at once?
- What price would make this the obvious choice even at double? There are no price points yet, so this is open.
- Was the AC contractor paid, and can it turn into a case study with a number in it?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one market and one buyer (e.g. Kuwait perfume brands on Instagram) and make the website, content and outreach all point at it.
2. Lead with one named offer, the store plus WhatsApp order agent, with a fixed scope, a delivery time, a published starting price and a guarantee the founder can afford. Keep SEO, GEO and apps as add-ons in the stack, not equal headline services.
3. Get proof: turn the AC client and one perfume brand into documented results (with permission) before scaling outreach.
4. Only use real scarcity, such as a cap based on actual weekly capacity.
