# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Effort for the buyer is close to zero.** "We write the text" from one short call, a preview link, two rounds of changes. The buyer does almost nothing.
- **Risk reversal is real.** No upfront payment and pay only when happy is stronger than most guarantees. The domain is in the client's name from day one, the care plan is optional and can be cancelled any month, and the site is the client's to move. Together these answer the "will I be trapped" fear.
- **The scarcity is honest.** "First 5 clients" is a true limit, not a fake countdown.
- **The niche is narrower.** The café page names a buyer and an outcome (table booking, click-and-collect, menu, Maps), and the AC booking case study is relevant proof of booking work.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Likelihood is still the weak part of the value equation.** There are no UK clients, no reviews, no results numbers, and the only real case study is anonymous with blurred screenshots, from Kuwait. The seller is a part-time freelancer on a Kuwait number with no UK presence. Pay-when-happy lowers the money risk, but it does not prove the café will get more bookings or orders.
2. **The market score is uncertain.** Independent cafés are easy to find on Instagram, but nobody has shown that they feel this as a big pain or have money to spend on it. Many already use free or cheap options: Google Business Profile, Instagram, delivery apps and booking widgets. Until that is tested, the market could pass this lens on its own.
3. **The dream outcome is vague and leans on price.** "Ready for Google" and "from £655" describe a deliverable and a price, not a result a café owner wants, like more covers or fewer missed orders. If the only difference a buyer sees is that it costs less, it is a commodity. The homepage is still general, which weakens the café pitch.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What result does a café owner measure: bookings, orders, calls? Can you promise any part of it?
- How many cafés already use a booking or ordering tool, and what does it cost them? (Open: no data yet.)
- What does "happy" mean in writing, and what happens to your time if a client never says it?
- How many hours a week can you give to delivery and support next to a full-time job? (Open.)
- What does it cost you to win one client through DMs? (Open.)
- Could you charge double and still be the obvious choice? If not, what is missing?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Rewrite the café offer around an outcome, for example "take table bookings and click-and-collect orders without phone calls". Stack each obstacle with its fix: menu updates, Google Business Profile set-up, a QR code for the table, and a staff walkthrough.
2. Get 1 to 2 real UK cafés live under the founding offer and publish their names, reviews and any before/after numbers, so likelihood is backed by proof.
3. Define "happy" in the written scope (it matches the agreed scope after 2 rounds of changes), and set a time limit on the review.
4. Test the market before scaling: track DM replies, calls booked and closes, and decide on the niche from those numbers.
