# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- There is now a small market to aim at: independent cafés, with a page of their own, a demo and an FAQ. The Monopoly lens wants a small market first, and this is the first sign of one.
- The booking case study is real know-how. Live availability, owner dashboard, technician jobs page, reminders and payments go well beyond a brochure site. If cafés need booking and ordering, this is the nearest thing Bracket has to a technical edge.
- Costs are low. One person working with AI agents means each project needs little cash, so Bracket can keep testing a niche for longer than a funded rival could.
- The terms (pay when happy, domain in the client's name, cancel the care plan any month) take away buyer risk. That helps win the first customers, but it does not make the position durable.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **A crowded market with nothing proprietary.** UK small-business websites are sold by local agencies, freelancers, offshore developers and DIY builders. Bracket has no network effect, no scale advantage and no brand yet, and "£655, ready for Google" is a small improvement, not a big leap. The likely result is a fight on price with thin margins.
2. **Distribution is unproven.** Cold Instagram DMs to cafés, from a Kuwait number, with no UK clients or reviews. Nobody knows what it costs to win one client, how many DMs that takes, or how many hours a week Bilal has next to a full-time job. A product with no repeatable way to reach buyers is not a business.
3. **Easy to copy and too broad to own.** The homepage still says "UK and internationally", and apps, AI agents and GEO are still listed. Any freelancer could copy the café page and the founding offer by month six. Unless Bracket owns one narrow segment, it never takes a big share of anything.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the secret: what do you know about independent cafés that the agencies selling to them do not?
- Why cafés, why Manchester, and why now? What is changing for cafés that makes them buy this year?
- How many independent cafés in Manchester lack online booking or ordering? This number is an open question. How many of them could Bracket realistically win?
- What stops a local agency from copying the café page and the pay-when-happy offer?
- Could the booking and ordering system become a reusable product for cafés, so each one is set up rather than built from scratch? That is where scale and lock-in could come from.
- How do the first 10 café clients arrive, and what does each one cost in hours?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Narrow the front door. Make the homepage about cafés (Manchester first) and push the other services well down the page.
2. Turn the booking and ordering build into one repeatable café product with a fixed setup, so each new client gets cheaper to serve and harder to replace.
3. Measure distribution: log DMs sent, replies, calls and signed clients for one month, along with the hours spent. Come back with the real cost to win one café.
4. Get one paying UK café live with a public review before widening the market.
